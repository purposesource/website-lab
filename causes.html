Causes and the Recipient List

Where the fees can go, who decides, and the list that names the recipients.

Recipient List: draft until the board adopts the first list · Updated 2026-10-28

Seven areas

  • HealthCare, prevention, medical supplies and the people who deliver them.
  • EducationAccess to learning, teachers, materials and opportunities to develop useful skills.
  • Poverty reliefFood security, livelihoods and support for people meeting their basic needs.
  • Humanitarian aidShelter, clean water, emergency response and help for displaced people.
  • EnvironmentProtecting and restoring the places people and other living things depend on.
  • Animal welfareCare, protection and better treatment of animals.
  • ResearchPublic-interest research whose findings can benefit people beyond a single organisation.

The Recipient List

Recipients must meet the published standard and appear on the board’s Recipient List. The board keeps the list outside the statutes and publishes it in versions. A removal for cause takes effect at once and is published with its ground; other changes take effect after thirty days’ public notice.

The Recipient Standard

Every recipient meets the floor in the statutes: a not-for-profit organisation with a charitable or public-benefit purpose in one of the seven areas; able to receive a transfer from a Swiss association lawfully; not under sanctions that apply to the Association; not controlled by anyone inside the Association; and paid only into a verified account in its own name.

The board’s Recipient Rules may add to the floor, never fall below it.

Held by: statutes Art. 7⁠ (live site)

Who chooses

Project administrators and contributors can choose among the seven areas, or leave their share to the Association. The choices are advisory and start in a trial period; the board decides.

Contributor choices can affect transfers only after at least a quarter of data and a published fairness and abuse review.
