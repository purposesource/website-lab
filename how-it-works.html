How it works

A project adopts the licence. Larger organisations that use the software take annual coverage. The Association passes the fees, after the payment provider’s fee and published, capped costs, to listed charities, and publishes every cost and transfer.

A summary; the licence and the statutes govern · Updated 2026-10-29

Share the code

A project chooses the licence for releases it has the authority to license. The source stays public, and the community keeps working in the repository.

The Association handles the payments and the public records. The project builds no billing system and runs no charity programme.

Adopt in five steps⁠

Larger users pay a published fee

An organisation counts as large if its whole group had 100 or more people, or US$1 million or more in revenue, in each member’s latest completed tax year. Below both, use is free.

Personal use is free. Non-profits, public education and public bodies never need coverage for their own use, whatever their size.

A larger organisation needs an Entitlement: Project, for one registered repository, or the Pass, for every registered repository. It pays an annual Purpose Fee.

The fees go to seven areas of care

Health, education, poverty relief, humanitarian aid, the environment, animal welfare and research.

After the payment provider’s fee and published, capped costs, the fees go to listed charities. Recipients must meet the published standard and appear on the board’s Recipient List. The first list is still to be published.

The people who build it have a say

Project administrators and contributors can choose among the seven areas, or leave their share to the Association. The choices are advisory and start in a trial period; the board decides.

Contributors are recognised for their work. Routed funds go to recipients, never to contributors or project owners.

A clear long-term path

Each release becomes available under Apache-2.0 four years after its publication. A later release starts its own clock, and nobody can extend the four years for anything already public.

A paid term permanently covers the code that is public by its end, a public commit included.

Purpose Source is source-available, not OSI-approved. Compare licensing models.
