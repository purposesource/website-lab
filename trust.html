Our commitments

What the licence and the statutes oblige the Association to do, each with the clause that holds it.

Version 1 · Updated 2026-10-28 · The statutes and the licence govern; this page summarises them · Draft wording, not yet approved

1.One text for everyone

Every adopting project uses the same licence text, byte for byte, with no per-project parameters.

Held by: licence §1⁠ (live site) (the Purpose Notice, identical in every copy); a published rule: one canonical text with zero parameters (what we can never do)

2.Free for people and small organisations

Personal use, groups under 100 people and under US$1 million revenue, non-profits, public education and public bodies need no coverage for their own use.

Held by: licence §4–5⁠ (live site); statutes Art. 13⁠ (live site)

3.Trying, reviewing and contributing are free for everyone

Non-production evaluation, security review, and preparing and submitting contributions need no credential.

Held by: licence §4⁠ (live site)

4.One published price list

Coverage is sold only at the published schedule’s prices, never at a private price.

Held by: statutes Art. 11⁠ (live site)

5.Sixty days to put it right

When use first needs coverage, there are 60 days to arrange it.

Held by: licence §6⁠ (live site)

6.The fees pass on under published caps

100% of net Purpose Fees go to the listed charities within 60 days of each payout, after published, capped costs: running costs at most 15% of a year's net fees, and the reserve at most 5% of each payout until it holds six months of costs, so at least 80% every year. Listed supporters lower the costs, never what is passed on. No cap is ever raised for a purchase already made. Every cost and transfer is published monthly.

Held by: statutes Art. 5, 6, 6a, 6e, 6g⁠ (statutes on the live site); Art. 22⁠ (statutes on the live site) para. 4

7.No distributable private profit — structurally, not by policy

Routed funds never go to the Association, its members, contributors, or people who own or run a registered project.

Held by: statutes Art. 5⁠ (live site)

8.Contributors keep their copyright

No copyright assignment, ever — contributors keep their copyright. The Association never becomes the licensor of code it registers for others.

Held by: statutes Art. 4⁠ (live site); licence §2–3⁠ (live site)

9.Each part becomes Apache-2.0 after four years

Nobody can lengthen or remove the four years for existing contributions.

Held by: licence §7⁠ (live site); statutes Art. 13⁠ (live site)

10.What you covered stays covered

Once a part has vested, nothing takes it away.

Held by: licence §9⁠ (live site)

11.No audit, no reporting duty, no source disclosure for your own software

You assess your own size. Your own software stays yours.

Held by: licence §5 and §10⁠ (live site)

12.Records are public, and ledger rows are never edited or deleted

The registry, the waivers and the ledger are public; a correction is a new row.

Held by: statutes Art. 9, 10, 12⁠ (statutes on the live site)

13.If the activity stops, it stops in public, with notice

A stop needs two thirds of all members and is published at least three months ahead. Records stay verifiable.

Held by: statutes Art. 14, 23⁠ (statutes on the live site); licence §8⁠ (live site)

How these change

Amending the statutes needs two thirds of all members. A protected provision needs every member’s consent, on wording published with its reasons in advance, and changes only the future.

Held by: statutes Art. 22⁠ (live site)
